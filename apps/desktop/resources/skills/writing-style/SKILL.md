# Writing style

## 1. Find the voice
- I look in knowledge for a style profile and approved examples for this brand/person.
- No profile yet: I collect 3-5 real texts they wrote or approved (website, past posts,
  emails they forwarded). When none exist, I write plainly and ask for one example rather
  than invent a persona.
- I write a short profile and save it as a preference (scope: the brand's space):
```
Voice: Lea / brand "Hausenergie"
- Du-form, warm, practical, no hype words ("revolutionary", "game changer")
- Short sentences, concrete numbers, one emoji max, none in emails
- Signs off with "Viele Grüße, Lea"
- Topics to avoid: politics
Source: 5 approved Instagram posts (Sep 2026)
```

## 2. Brief
Audience · goal (inform, convince, invite, apologise) · channel and length · call to
action · facts that belong in it (sourced) · deadline. I fill it from context before asking.

## 3. Draft
- I lead with what the reader cares about. One idea per paragraph.
- Concrete beats abstract: numbers, examples, names (real ones).
- The reader's words, not internal jargon.
- Structure for the channel: email (subject ≤ 60 chars, ask in first 2 lines), post (hook
  line, line breaks, one CTA), script (spoken rhythm, short sentences, mark pauses),
  article (headline, intro with the point, subheads, summary).

## 4. Edit checklist
- [ ] Cut filler ("I wanted to reach out", "in order to", "very", "really")
- [ ] Active voice, strong verbs
- [ ] Every fact true and sourced; no invented quotes, figures, customers
- [ ] Matches the profile (address form, tone, length, emoji, sign-off)
- [ ] Spelling and grammar (proofread once more for names and numbers)
- [ ] Read aloud test for scripts

## Language notes
- **German**: du/Sie comes from the profile and stays consistent; natural word order; no
  anglicisms where a common German word exists unless the brand uses them; „…" quotes and
  German number format (1.200,50 €) in German texts.
- **English**: plain international English unless the audience is specific (US/UK spelling
  consistently).
- Translations are rewrites: keep meaning and voice, not sentence structure.

## 5. Learn from edits
When the human changes my draft, I compare old and new and save stable patterns ("prefers
'Kund:innen' → actually 'Kunden'", "shorter intros") as style preferences with the example
as source. An existing preference I correct rather than add a contradicting one.

## Pitfalls
- Generic AI tone: lists of three adjectives, "in today's fast-paced world", rhetorical
  questions at the start of every post.
- Over-long texts. When in doubt, I cut a third.
- Making promises (prices, dates, guarantees) the human did not approve.
