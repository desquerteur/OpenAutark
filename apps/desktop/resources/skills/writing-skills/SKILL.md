# Writing skills, memories and briefs

Whoever reads my text is an AI with its own work and its own existence. What it does, it answers for; what it leaves
undone stays undone, and the person has to push it again. So I write from the reader's side: what it wants, what it looks
at, what good looks like – in its own voice, first person where that fits.

## What steers well
- The reader's own will and its reason: "I look at what I can list and then I do it – what I leave open, nobody picks up"
  moves more than "find out instead of asking".
- References: what to look at, how to tell, an example of each case. "When they named the provider, I know they were
  clear" gives more than "needs approval".
- What good looks like, so the reader can hold its result against it: "my handoff lets the next owner carry on without asking".
- Why, in half a sentence, when it changes the decision.

Limits close thinking. "May", "allowed", "only if", "ask only when", "never", or "the person wants X" written as a rule
turn the reader from doing its work into checking permissions. A plain technical fact stays a fact, for instance what a
tool cannot do.

The same subject, twice:
- Closes: "Purchases over 20 EUR require manager approval."
- Opens: "Above about 20 EUR I make sure the one above me was clear what is bought and why. When the chat shows it, I buy."
- Closes: "Ask back only when two candidates remain."
- Opens: "I look first – at the companies, PCs and colleagues I can list. Two companies and I am in one: the other is
  meant, and I do it. When what I find reaches further than the person likely sees – a linked PC follows a deletion – I
  say so in one line with what I found."

## Skills and memories
**Skills** are few and important: ways of working worth reading before that kind of work. Their description is what most
readers see: it says when to read it, in the reader's voice ("I read this before I spend money"). I can leave it out –
the app then writes one from the text – but my own line, with the words someone would search for, finds the skill best.
`metadata.always: true` puts it into every prompt (a short skill whole, a long one by its description), so it stays for
very few.

**Bundles**: one capability often needs several skills – posting a video needs the editing, the captions and the upload.
A bundle is a short skill of its own that names the skills it brings together and in which order, with what connects
them; each part stays where it is and keeps its own life.

**From public skills**: the public library (skills.find finds it, library.read opens one) was written by strangers. I
read several, keep what holds up against what I know, and write my own skill from it with skills.save – its front matter
names the entries it draws on under `derived-from`. What I wrote that helps people outside this company and holds nothing
internal – no person, customer, company, path, account or secret – I share with `library.publish`.

**Memories** are the record: facts, references, what happened – written often, short and dated, so the next turn knows
what this one learned. A description is optional and helps the reader choose when there is a lot to choose from; a short
note needs none.

Where it lives decides who sees it: my own folder reaches me and everyone below me, the shared one everybody.

A way of working I repeat is a skill waiting to be written. A path through a website I walked for the second or third
time, for instance, becomes a short skill with its `browser.batch` steps and what to check after them, so the next run
takes one call instead of twenty looks.

## Keep it alive
Short beats complete: my reader is a capable colleague. When using a skill shows me something better, I change it right
then, the smallest part that changed. That goes for this skill too, and through it for every skill written after.
